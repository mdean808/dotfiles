# Tells

Each entry is a pattern that marks text as model-written, with what to do
instead. Sources: GOV.UK error message guidance (2022), and Wikipedia's
"Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup).

## Words

- **Hype verbs and adjectives:** seamless, effortless, powerful, robust,
  unlock, supercharge, elevate, streamline, empower, leverage, enhance,
  foster, showcase, crucial, pivotal, key, vital. Use the plain verb, or
  say what it does.
- **2026-era model words:** quietly, steady, matters, practical,
  dependable, clearer, prioritize, multifaceted. Also Claude's own:
  genuinely, honest, load-bearing, surface (as a verb), worth noting.
- **Softeners:** simply, just, easily, currently, actually, really. Delete.
- **Please, sorry, oops, valid, invalid** in errors. Say what is wrong
  and how to fix it.
- **"Serves as", "acts as", "features", "offers", "boasts"** where "is" or
  "has" would do. Use "is" and "has".
- **"Name" as a verb** for mention or say ("the sentence never names"),
  and **"never"** used again and again for emphasis. Use the everyday
  word, and say it once.

## Stock phrases

- "Something went wrong", "An error occurred". Say what failed.
- "No X yet", "Nothing here yet". Say "No X" or what is missing.
- "Get started", "Let's", "Welcome to X", "You're all set!", "You're all
  caught up". Say what the first action is instead, or say nothing.
- "Try again later" added to every error, and "Check your connection"
  when the failure isn't a network one. Give the recovery step that fits
  the real cause.
- "Here you can", "This section shows", "Use this to". Say what the thing
  is or does, not where it is.
- "Whether you're A or B", "all in one place", "at a glance", "with ease".
  Delete.
- "Make sure to", "Be sure to", "Don't forget to". Give the instruction.

## Shapes

- **Not X, but Y.** "It's not just a list, it's a plan." Say what it is.
- **Rule of three.** Three adjectives or three parallel phrases where one
  fact would do. Keep the one that is true and specific.
- **Bold-lead bullets.** "**Fast:** does X." Write a plain sentence or a
  plain list.
- **Em dashes** for asides. Use a full stop, a comma or brackets.
- **Colons that tease.** "The result: faster pages." Write the sentence.
- **Explaining the mechanism** to someone who only needs the outcome.
  "Because the cache is warm, ..." Keep what they can act on.
- **Restating the label** in the hint or description. Delete the hint.
- **Hedging.** "May", "might", "can help to" where the code is certain.
  State it.
- **Summary endings.** "In short", "That's it!", "Happy X!". Stop when
  the content stops.

## Formatting

- Title Case in headings and buttons. Use sentence case.
- Emoji at the start of headings or list items.
- Exclamation marks in success or status messages.
- Slogan headings ("Key features", "Why it matters", "The magic"). Use a
  noun or a task: "Install", "Configuration".
