# Tells

Each entry is a pattern that marks text as model-written, with what to
write instead. Some entries come from GOV.UK's error message guidance
(2022) and Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" page.

## Words

- Hype verbs and adjectives, such as seamless, effortless, powerful,
  robust, unlock, supercharge, elevate, streamline, empower, leverage,
  enhance, foster, showcase, crucial, pivotal, key and vital. Use the
  plain verb, or say what the thing does.
- Words models overuse, such as quietly, steady, matters, practical,
  dependable, clearer, prioritize and multifaceted. Claude also overuses
  genuinely, honest, load-bearing, worth noting and "surface" as a verb.
  Use a plain word, or cut the word.
- Softeners, such as simply, just, easily, currently, actually and
  really. Delete them.
- "Please", "sorry", "oops", "valid" and "invalid" in errors. Say what is
  wrong and how to fix it.
- "Serves as", "acts as", "features", "offers" and "boasts". Use "is" or
  "has".
- "Name" as a verb for "mention" or "say", as in "the sentence never
  names the file". Use the everyday word.
- "Never" used again and again for emphasis. Say it once.

## Stock phrases

- "Something went wrong" and "An error occurred". Say what failed.
- "No X yet" and "Nothing here yet". Say "No X", or say what is missing.
- "Get started", "Let's", "Welcome to X", "You're all set!" and "You're
  all caught up". Say what the first action is, or say nothing.
- "Try again later" on every error, and "Check your connection" when the
  failure isn't a network one. Give the recovery step that fits the real
  cause.
- "Here you can", "This section shows" and "Use this to". Say what the
  thing is or does, not where it is.
- "Whether you're A or B", "all in one place", "at a glance" and "with
  ease". Delete them.
- "Make sure to", "Be sure to" and "Don't forget to". Give the
  instruction.

## Shapes

- Saying what a thing isn't before what it is, as in "It's not just a
  list, it's a plan." Say what the thing is.
- The rule of three: three adjectives or three parallel phrases where
  one fact would do. Keep the one that is true and specific.
- Bullets that open with a bold label, such as "**Fast:** does X."
  Write a plain sentence or a plain list.
- Em dashes for asides. Use a full stop, a comma or brackets.
- Colons that tease, as in "The result: faster pages." Write the
  sentence.
- An explanation of the mechanism for a reader who needs the outcome,
  such as "Because the cache is warm, ...". Keep what the reader can act
  on.
- A hint or description that restates the label. Delete it.
- Hedges such as "may", "might" and "can help to" where the fact is
  certain. State the fact.
- Summary endings, such as "In short", "That's it!" and "Happy X!". Stop
  when the content stops.

## Formatting

- Title Case in headings and buttons. Use sentence case.
- Emoji at the start of headings or list items. Delete them.
- Exclamation marks in success or status messages. Use a full stop, or
  no mark on a short message.
- Slogan headings, such as "Key features", "Why it matters" and "The
  magic". Use a noun or a task: "Install", "Configuration".
